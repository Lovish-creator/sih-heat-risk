# Research Review: Thermal Stress & Heat-Health Evidence

This document reviews key peer-reviewed literature connecting biometeorological thermal metrics (UTCI, WBGT, Heat Index) to human health outcomes, mortality, and occupational safety in Indian cities.

| Paper | Location | Data | Metric | Finding | Relevance to SIH26083 | Scientific Limitation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Azhar et al. (2014)** *PLOS ONE* | Ahmedabad, Gujarat | AMC Daily all-cause mortality registries (2009-2011), IMD synoptic weather data | Daily Max Temperature ($T_{\max}$), Heatwave thresholds | During the May 2010 heatwave ($T_{\max} = 46.8^\circ\text{C}$), an excess of **1,344 all-cause deaths** occurred ($\sim 43\%$ increase over baseline), disproportionately affecting infants and elderly ($65+$). | Justifies why Ahmedabad was selected as the pilot city; establishes empirical evidence that extreme heat produces immediate, measurable mortality spikes. | Used dry-bulb temperature only; did not model radiative heat load ($T_{mrt}$) or humidity interactions. |
| **Kotharkar & Ghosh (2022)** *Urban Climate* | Multi-city India (Nagpur, Delhi, Ahmedabad, Kolkata) | Landsat LST, MODIS, Synoptic weather stations, Local Climate Zones (LCZ) | Surface Urban Heat Island (SUHI), Humidex, UTCI | Urban canopy and dense built environments elevate nighttime temperatures by $2\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$, severely hindering human physiological nocturnal cooling recovery. | Grounds the inclusion of population density and duration multipliers in our multi-criteria risk model. | Satellite LST represents skin temperature of roofs and roads, not human-level 2m air temperature ($T_a$). |
| **Di Napoli et al. (2018)** *Int. J. Biometeorol.* | Global / European cities | ERA5 Reanalysis, UTCI biometeorological model, Mortality time series | UTCI ($^\circ\text{C}$), Heat Index, PET | UTCI demonstrates stronger statistical correlation with thermal discomfort and heat-related excess mortality than simple air temperature, particularly during humid/radiative extremes. | Validates our core thesis: UTCI provides a superior representation of physiological strain ("What weather does to humans") compared to $T_a$ alone. | High computational complexity of full Fiala multi-node heat balance model requires polynomial operational approximation. |
| **Venugopal et al. (2020)** *Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health* | Chennai & Southern India | Occupational field measurements, WBGT wet-bulb globe monitors, worker surveys | WBGT ($^\circ\text{C}$), Heart rate, Core body temperature | Outdoor construction, agricultural, and brick-kiln workers exposed to $WBGT > 30^\circ\text{C}$ experienced heavy physiological strain, dehydration, and acute kidney injury risks. | Justifies incorporating dedicated NIOSH WBGT calculations and specific outdoor worker advisories and rest-work regimens. | Microclimatic variations at specific job sites require portable on-body sensors for pinpoint precision. |
| **Gasparrini et al. (2015)** *The Lancet* | 384 locations across 12 countries | Multi-country daily time-series mortality data and temperature records | Distributed Lag Non-linear Models (DLNM), Relative Risk (RR) | Non-linear, U-shaped exposure-response curve with mortality risk escalating exponentially beyond the Minimum Mortality Temperature (MMT). | Grounds our choice of a **Relative Mortality/Health Risk Score (0-100)** rather than fabricating ungrounded linear death counts. | Transferability of MMT curves across cities requires localized multi-year daily mortality calibration datasets (Tier-3). |
